Acknowledgement of Country

Choose whether to include an Acknowledgement of Country for your event.

What is an Acknowledgement of Country?

An Acknowledgement of Country recognises Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples as the Traditional Custodians of Country and respects their continuing connections to land, waters, culture and community. Thoughtful acknowledgements contribute to reconciliation by encouraging recognition, respect and learning.

How is it different from a Welcome to Country? An Acknowledgement can be offered by anyone. A Welcome to Country is given by a Traditional Owner of the Country where the event takes place, or someone authorised to do so. An Acknowledgement is not a substitute for a Welcome to Country.

Build a meaningful acknowledgement

  1. Research whose Country your specific venue is on. Check local Traditional Owner organisations, councils and Land Councils; don't assume one name covers an entire city.
  2. Confirm the preferred name and spelling using reliable local sources. If sources disagree, seek guidance rather than guessing.
  3. Consider why recognising Country matters for your event. Acknowledge the continuing connection of Traditional Custodians to Country and pay respects to Elders past and present.
  4. Write in your own respectful words, using a Traditional Custodian name only when you have verified it.

Suggested structure: “We acknowledge [verified Traditional Custodians] as the Traditional Custodians of the Country where [event] is held. We recognise their continuing connection to Country and pay our respects to Elders past and present.” Adapt this thoughtfully; it is a starting point, not a substitute for research.

Where to research Traditional Custodians

Use these resources alongside local Traditional Owner organisations and councils. No single national map can reliably establish the Traditional Custodians of every street address.

  • Reconciliation Australia: Acknowledgement and Welcome to Country
  • AIATSIS: Whose Country am I on?
  • AIATSIS: Map of Indigenous Australia (indicative, not exact boundaries)
  • Indigenous.gov.au (government information and links)
Common mistakes and cultural considerations
  • Do not guess Traditional Custodian names or assume a capital-city reference applies to every suburb.
  • Do not treat indicative maps as precise land boundaries or ignore contested identifications.
  • Check spelling, terminology and pronunciation; ask an appropriate local organisation when uncertain.
  • Do not describe an Acknowledgement as a Welcome to Country or claim to speak on behalf of Traditional Owners.
  • Avoid tokenistic copy-and-paste statements. Consider the event, its location and your responsibilities to Country.
